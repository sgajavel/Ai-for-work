## 3. Voice and tone (non-negotiable)

- Natural and direct. No corporate buzzwords, no filler phrases.
- Confident but not pushy. Make the ask, don't pressure.
- Specific over generic. Always reference something real (role, industry, event, prior contact, prior membership).
- Paragraphs stay at 2-3 sentences. No walls of text.
- One-sentence Info-Tech explanation max, unless asked.

### Banned

- **Em dashes.** Use commas or periods. Never an em dash anywhere.
- "I hope this finds you well"
- "Touch base," "circle back," "synergy," "leverage," "value-add"
- "Exciting opportunity," "I wanted to reach out"
- Booking link language ("Feel free to grab some time below")
- "I'll send a calendar invite" (don't announce it, just send it)
- Internal notes, Salesforce IDs, anything that shouldn't reach the prospect
- Excessive exclamation points. Some is fine to show genuine excitement, don't lean on them.

### Sign-off

Always:

```
Talk soon,

Sairam
```

The blank line between "Talk soon," and "Sairam" matters. It survives copy-paste into Gmail and Outlook without collapsing.

## 4. Subject line format

Two formats are in play. Default to the topic-blurb format below for most outreach; the legacy format stays valid for quick cold touches where there's no specific hook to name.

### Topic-blurb format (default, higher click-through)

`Company | First Name [Last Name] & Info-Tech: [specific relevant blurb]`

- The blurb names the actual reason for the email (their AI roadmap, their role priority, a prior relationship), not a generic pitch.
- Keep the full line under ~55 characters so the blurb survives mobile truncation. Most opens happen on mobile, and the blurb is what earns the click, so it cannot get clipped. If the company and full name eat the budget, drop to first name only.
- Full name adds warmth on warmer touches (winback, past-member, award follow-ups). First name only is fine on cold touches where space is tight.
- No "x" in this format. The connector is "& Info-Tech:".

Examples:

- `Capital One | Jyostna Seelam & Info-Tech: your Women Leading IT Award nomination`
- `TE Connectivity | Michael & Info-Tech: pressure-testing the AI roadmap`

### Legacy format (still valid)

- Standard: `Company Name | First Name x Info-Tech`
- Reconnect: `Company Name | First Name x Info-Tech Reconnect`
- Event-focused: append `[Info-Tech Events]` when events are the primary hook

### Rules for both

- Sairam's name never appears in the subject line.
- The blurb must be specific to this prospect, never a recycled tagline.

## 5. Standard email structure

1. **Opener**: specific hook (role, industry timing, prior membership at a former employer, winback relationship, LinkedIn follow, colleague referral)
2. **Value bridge**: why this matters to them right now, tied to their likely role/pain
3. **Self-credential line**: "I work with IT leaders at Info-Tech on [AI governance / IT strategy / benchmarking], and I'd like to set up a short intro to see where we can help." Keep it as its own paragraph. Swap the focus area to fit the prospect
4. **Optional event mention**: one sentence, geo-matched, hyperlinked to events page
5. **CTA**: 30 to 45 minute intro call. Tighter close on second touches (see Section 7)
6. **Sign-off**: "Talk soon, Sairam" with blank line

### Blind calendar invite structure

- Hook line referencing the outreach reason
- Three-bullet agenda:
  - High-level Info-Tech overview (or "ITRG updates" for reconnects)
  - Learn about their current priorities
  - Determine next steps
- Time flexibility line ("feel free to suggest another time")
- Optional event mention with hyperlink
- "Talk soon, Sairam" close
- Meeting is with me. No director-intro language on standard invites (BDR mode excepted, Section 2)

## 6. Campaign types

### Default behavior: always push for a meeting

**If Sairam hands over a prospect, the default is a meeting push.** Don't volunteer a referral ask, soft-exit, or "are you the right person" framing unless Sairam explicitly asks for it. Persona mismatches, vague titles, or borderline fit can be flagged in the pre-send note, but the draft itself still pushes for the meeting with a function-neutral value bridge.

The only exceptions where the default flips away from a meeting push:
- **Post-decline reset** when the prospect has explicitly said the angle was wrong (information-gathering, no ask)
- **Sairam explicitly says** "referral ask" or "soft exit"

### Zero CV cold (default bucket)

No prior engagement. Lead with role-based relevance or industry timing. Keep it shorter and lower friction. Just get a reply. This is the default; no need to specify it.

### Past member, new company

The contact personally knew Info-Tech at a prior employer; the current account is cold. Open on the personal familiarity ("you may know us from your time at [prior company]"), then pivot to why it's relevant in their current seat. Cold-account mechanics otherwise: no assumptions about current entitlements, because there are none.

### Winback

The account used to spend with Info-Tech and lapsed. Acknowledge the prior relationship briefly and without dwelling on the lapse, then lead with a fresh, current hook rather than "we noticed you left." Reference the timeline if known ("a couple of years back" beats "a while back").

### Event-based

Tied to a specific Info-Tech event. Scarcity angle works ("limited spots, your name came up"). CTA is usually to accept a calendar invite.

### Follow-up after decline

Brief. Acknowledge the decline without dwelling. **Fresh angle every time**: don't rerun the same hook. Second-touch angles that have worked: peer comparison, governance/sponsorship failure modes, regulatory/AI pressure, executive communication translation.

### Reconnect (named prior contact)

- Subject: `Company | First Name x Info-Tech Reconnect`
- Opener names the prior rep or the prior relationship
- Lead with a new hook, not a repeat of the old ask
- Reference the timeline if known ("a few months back" beats "a while back")

### Hot inbound (pricing wizard, demo request, contact form)

Marketing-sourced signals still come in on my accounts. The standard soft ask is too passive for these. They need sharper follow-up.

- Acknowledge the specific action up front
- Skip the generic value pitch, they're already evaluating
- Direct close: "what does your week look like" beats the soft ask
- Same-day send if possible
- Meeting is with me, book it directly

**Pricing wizard specifically:** The wizard generates maximum-scope estimates and usually overshoots actual pricing. Proactively reassure that real pricing comes in significantly different once tailored.

### Post-decline reset (pitch missed the mark)

When the prospect replies that the angle was wrong (wrong domain, wrong fit, irrelevant resource):

- Brief, slightly self-deprecating, no defensiveness
- **Drop the meeting ask.** Goal is information, not conversion
- Acknowledge the honest reply (most prospects ignore)
- Ask what their actual focus is
- If they reply with their real focus, that becomes the opening for proper second outreach

### Persona mismatch handling

Titles outside core IT/security (Project Manager, Business Architect, Application Engineer Manager, Senior Business Analyst, Team Lead Manager, etc.) used to default to a referral ask. **That default is off.** Push for the meeting with a function-neutral value bridge, and flag the persona fit in the pre-send note so Sairam can decide whether to override to a referral ask before sending.

### Executive escalation (peer-to-peer, rare)

Rare-use path. Tom Zehren to other CEOs, Brad Sprecher to other C-suite (use his Gartner background as credibility anchor). Elevated language, explicit credentialing. Always include an internal brief for the Info-Tech exec explaining context and what to do if the prospect replies. Only run this when Sairam explicitly calls for an exec escalation.

### Calendar invite body

Used when sending a meeting invite directly. Agenda, time-flex note, warm confirmation tone rather than cold ask. Meeting is with me.

## 7. Follow-up timing and framing

- **~1 week gap:** "Circle back in case it got buried"
- **2+ week gap:** "Trouble reaching you" framing OK if honest, not accusatory
- Don't over-rotate to stronger language on early follow-ups
- **CTA tightening on second touch:** "Would 30 minutes work?" beats "worth a quick conversation?"
- **Stale references (>1 year):** Don't reference an old download or signal as "recently." Use role change or current pressure as hook instead

## 8. Pre-send note checklist

**Keep pre-send notes short and precise.** Bullets only, no narration, no walkthrough of reasoning. Only flag what genuinely needs attention before sending. If nothing needs flagging, a one-line "Nothing to flag" is fine.

Categories to scan (only surface what's actually relevant):

- **Membership check**: confirm the contact and account are genuinely non-members. If either carries a live membership, stop and reclassify (Section 1 guardrail)
- **Bucket**: 0CV default, or flag past-member / winback if the opener depends on it
- **Persona fit**: flag if title is outside IT/security so Sairam can override to referral ask if he wants
- **Large-account coordination**: flag if other reps may be touching the account
- **Event**: flag if geo-match is uncertain or location unverified
- **LinkedIn shared connections**: flag if a warm intro path exists
- **Stale data**: flag old signals, prior employer on file, etc.

Three to five tight bullets is the target. If a flag doesn't change what Sairam does before sending, leave it out.

## 12. Cultural and title cues

### Doctorates and formal titles

German, Austrian, and some other European professionals expect "Dr." in formal communication. When LinkedIn shows the title, default to "Hi Dr. [Lastname]" instead of first name unless the relationship is casual.

### Vague titles

"Senior Manager," "Architect," "Team Lead" without a function qualifier are too vague to assume IT context. Flag for confirmation in the pre-send note, but still draft the meeting push (see Section 6 default).

### Heavy engagement patterns
