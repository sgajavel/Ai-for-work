## Key Learnings

### Pivoting to Proposal (Stage 3 entry gate)
- You only pivot to Proposal once the QP formula (VMd x V x P x P) is fully built out: OAT tool is a yes to everything, and all Power and Plan questions in the decision tree are answered.
- Before Proposal stage you can talk pricing in ranges, but a physical proposal only goes out once the opportunity is formally moved into Proposal stage.
- Proposal stage entrance/exit criteria: enters once the proposal presentation is done and pricing has been socialized (not negotiated). Exits once the proposal is submitted, price and terms are verbally agreed by the DM, CPQ is built and delivered, a MAP with reverse timeline is documented in writing, and procurement/legal timelines are understood.

### Plan & Next Steps (the close-the-loop moment)
- The trigger question: "Now that you understand how Info-Tech would work with you on your critical initiatives, are you interested in moving forward with a partnership?" This maps closely to Jillian's development item 2 (earn the close before booking next steps), phrased here as a hard yes/no gate.
- If "Yes": run the Power questions (who else is involved, sign-off limits, budget owner, veto power, legal/procurement/finance involvement) and Plan questions (what steps, who to introduce you to, timeline for procurement/legal review).
- If "No": treated as a signal you're not connected to a true business priority. The fix is to go back to VMD and re-confirm business goals, not push harder on logistics.

**Power questions (verbatim from deck):**
- Who else will be involved in the decision?
- Is there anyone who will need to sign-off once the decision is made?
- Would this be coming out of your budget or is there someone else who controls the budget?
- Do you have a sign-off limit that you'd like to stay under?
- How do you typically make these types of partnership decisions?
- How have purchases like this been made in the past?
- Will you need to seek approval from anyone else in the organization?
- Who else would be impacted by this decision?
- Who could potentially veto this decision?
- Will we need to meet with legal? Procurement? Finance? Other departments?
- "So let me play this back to you. It sounds like [insert role/name] will need to be brought into the conversation? Is that right? Is there anyone else who needs to be involved?"

**Plan questions (verbatim from deck):**
- What steps do you suggest we take moving forward?
- Would you be willing to introduce us to the main person (use names if possible) in Procurement so we can get the process started?
- Is there anyone else we should include in our next steps?
- When do you think we can meet with [insert role/name]?
- How much time should we allow for procurement/legal review?
- Is there anything else you think you'd need to see before moving forward? Or, is it fair to say that if we complete the steps we've outlined in this list, you will be in a position to move forward with an agreement?

### Commit Phase (Stage 4)
- Goal: secure signature and PO to generate invoice.
- Key actions: align with procurement/legal for final approvals, establish contract execution timeline, update SFDC.
- Common failure points flagged: not understanding procurement/legal timelines, not questioning vacations (accounting for people being out when setting timelines), and veto power surfacing late if it wasn't squashed earlier in Power questioning.
- Entrance criteria: negotiation complete, proposal agreed upon, verbal/email commitment obtained.
- Exit criteria: signed agreement and/or PO, onboarding call invite scheduled, counselor assigned.

### Account Transition Process
(Relevant mainly if you inherit or hand off SMB accounts)
- Applies when Acquire sells a new account and it transitions to an Engage rep; onboarding must happen within 30 days (Service Kick-Off).
- Sequence: Transition Form (completed by outgoing rep before internal meeting) → Internal Transition Meeting (outgoing rep, CSM, incoming rep, before client meeting) → Warm Hand-Off Call (outgoing, CSM optional, incoming rep, AND the client DM) → Email introductions (outgoing rep sends, incoming rep CC'd).
- For brand new accounts, the Acquire rep is not needed on the Warm Hand-Off call. The point is to move fast while the new member is still excited.
- Messaging for existing accounts under a rep change: frame it as "added to your account management team," not a handoff, to minimize disruption. Sample line: "I want to introduce you to Paul. Paul has been added to your account management team as he brings great experience in your industry."
