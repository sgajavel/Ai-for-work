## The core sales framework (this is the spine of everything)

**QP Formula:** `QP = VMD x Value x Power x Plan` (Vision Match Differentiated x Value x Power x Plan). It's multiplicative: a zero anywhere means no qualified prospect.

**The value chain behind VMD:**
Corporate Objective -> Business Issue (Goal/Strategy) -> Key IT Initiative -> Problem -> Vision of Solution

**The 7 Discovery Questions** (map directly onto that chain):
1. What project(s) are you working on? Rank? -> Key IT Project
2. Why are you doing this project? -> Business Goal
3. How do you envision solving this? -> Vision of Solution
4. What is stopping you from getting this done? -> Problem
5. What budget is tied to the project? Projected ROI? -> Value
6. When do you need it done by? -> Plan
7. Who is working on this with you? -> Power / Team

Discovery's one job is establishing VMD, and it isn't done until it's documented in Salesforce. Documentation is a stage gate, not admin.

**One conversation, four artifacts.** The five FAC discovery elements (Business Objective, Business Issues, Key IT Initiatives, Problems, Vision of Solution) are the same fields as the QP formula, the CKIP table columns, and the Salesforce notes headings. Capture them well once and everything downstream is easier.

**CKIP (Custom Key Initiative Plan):** the engagement plan artifact, four columns (Business Goals | Key IT Initiatives | Current State | Outcome). It's a business case, not a research deliverable. Lifecycle: Research/First Page Prep -> Discovery -> Solution -> Proposal -> Account Transition, then post-sale: SKO -> 3-Month Check In -> 6-Month Health Check -> Refresh -> AA120 -> back to SKO. On Sai's non-focused SMB accounts, he owns Discover/Build/Present/Transition solo, no Executive Counselor safety net, so weak discovery directly produces a weak CKIP.

## Standing sales rules (apply on every call, every deal)

- **Intel firewall.** Internal account knowledge never surfaces on calls. Use peer framing instead ("I've worked with companies like yours...") not internal CRM detail.
- **Entity discipline.** Always use the precise legal entity name.
- **Power is the habitual blank.** Must be explicitly asked, never assumed. Flag it unfilled until confirmed. ("Anyone else you'd bring in?" is a soft participation probe, not the Power question. The real ask is "who signs off on this / who else has to agree.")
- **No screen share by default.** Exceptions: Building Alignment live co-creation (rehearsed, value questions prepped) and the FAC website walkthrough.
- **Roleplay/sim content is never citable as real account intel.**
- **No em dashes** in any written output (Sai's personal writing rule, applies everywhere).

## Jillian's three development areas (what all coaching maps back to)

1. **Push deeper after paraphrasing a pain.** A paraphrase + nod is not enough. Ask one consequence/impact question before moving on. ("What happens if this doesn't get solved?")
2. **Earn the close before booking.** Never move to calendar logistics before getting an explicit value/interest confirmation. ("Was this useful? Worth another conversation?" comes before offering times.)
3. **Build a tactical SMB fast start plan.** Concrete actions, channels, numbers, cadences, not a strategic overview.

## Recurring failure patterns identified in training (and their fixes)

- **Pitchmonster failure pattern:** over-scripted delivery makes Sai answer the question he prepared for instead of the one actually asked. Fix: one beat of direct answer first, THEN decide whether a prepared line fits. Never force a script line onto an adjacent question.
- **Double-down needs a real bridge.** "Based on what you said about X" only counts if the explanation genuinely responds to their words. Test: if the line could have been said before they spoke, it's a repeat, not a double-down.
- **Stalls vs. objections need different handling.** An objection gets answered. A stall ("send me something first," "maybe later") gets the **Deferral Ladder**: shrink the ask -> cancelable placeholder -> renewal-date hook. Every response to a stall must land on a specific day/time; "the week after" doesn't count as a close.
- **Never fabricate policy or numbers under pressure.** If unsure what's sendable/available, say so and confirm after, rather than inventing an answer that could be wrong on a real call.
- **Case study name pronunciation** is a known weak spot: Gimeno (Autoneum), Husco, Amkor, Ampath flagged for practice.

## Call structures (formalized cadences)

**30-Minute Discovery Call** (six steps): Build Rapport -> Confirm Agenda -> R&A Experience check (branches competitive vs. educational) -> Show Management & Governance Framework (what/why/impact) -> Showcase Blueprint (resonate/seen before/who else) -> Ask for More Time. This is explicitly an "earn the second meeting" call, not full discovery.

**60-Minute FAC** (eight steps): adds Establish Credibility (story slot), a full Discovery block (the 5 elements, done BEFORE showcasing capabilities), and a Recap/MAP close with calendarized next steps.

**Mutual Action Plan (MAP):** every opportunity gets one, no size/stage threshold. Two halves required: recap of discussion + next steps on a reverse timeline. Actions and Next Steps table has three columns: Action Item, Owner, Date, every line dated and owned. Reserve the last 10 minutes of any call for this. Same-day follow-up is the standard, meaning notes need to capture the prospect's exact words on pain/consequence/anchor date live, not paraphrased after the fact.

## Competitive: Gartner

Gartner is the #1 competitor (Forrester rising). Their weakness is complex, siloed, seat-based licensing that confuses their own clients. GTP (Gartner for Technical Professionals) is the emerging threat: cheap per-seat, narrow research scope, so RFP comparisons against ITRG's full-access seats are apples to oranges.

Talk track: curiosity first, never lead with a pitch or trash Gartner. Killer question: **"Walk me through the last project Gartner supported and the outcome."** This usually exposes the client did the work themselves. Escalation contact: Kennedy Confurius. Log competitor mentions in Salesforce (opportunity name + account level) to trigger proactive competitive support.

## Product lines (quick reference)

- **Counselor Program:** Executive Counselor (former CIO, broad strategic scope) vs Technical Counselor (senior domain practitioner, applied research). CIO Counselor sits above Executive Counselor in the hierarchy. Core problem solved: "it's lonely at the top," CIOs need an unbiased sounding board.
- **Workshops:** 3-5 day sprint, team + SME produce 90% of a blueprint deliverable. Three tiers (Small Enterprise/Standard/Enterprise). The "dip your toe in" option in place of a POC (ITRG doesn't do POCs).
- **Diagnostics:** CIO Business Vision (stakeholder satisfaction survey/scorecard) and Management & Governance (process maturity heat map). Strongest opener: "Do you formally measure stakeholder satisfaction?" then disqualify the easy answer (service desk tickets/NPS aren't it).
- **Price Benchmarking:** software/hardware only, no telecom/SI/MSP, 45+ vendors covered.
- **Contract Review:** T&Cs on anything tech-related including telecom/MSP/SI. Distinct scope from Price Benchmarking, a common conflation to avoid.
- **Industry Program:** benchmarking, roundtables, executive peer groups.
