## The progression

```
Discovery / FAC
  -> Plan & Next Steps gate   (yes/no, the close-the-loop moment)
  -> Proposal   (Stage 3)
  -> Commit     (Stage 4)
  -> Account Transition / SKO
```

---

## The Plan & Next Steps gate

This is the hinge. One question, asked plainly, answered yes or no.

> **"Now that you understand how Info-Tech would work with you on your critical
> initiatives, are you interested in moving forward with a partnership?"**

**This is Jillian development area 2 in its concrete form.** Info-Tech's own framework
treats the value confirmation and the next-step booking as a **single gate**, not two
separate motions. Do not move to logistics before this question is answered.

### If yes

Run the Power questions, then the Plan questions. Both banks are below.

### If no

A no here is not a scheduling problem and it is not an objection to handle. It is a
signal that **the conversation is not connected to a real business priority.**

The fix is to go back to VMD and re-confirm business goals. Do not push harder on
logistics. Pushing logistics against a no is how a cycle dies quietly.

## Power question bank (verbatim from the deck)

Power is the habitual blank across the whole motion. It is never assumed, always
asked. This is the canonical list.

**Who decides**

- Who else will be involved in the decision?
- Is there anyone who will need to sign-off once the decision is made?
- How do you typically make these types of partnership decisions?
- How have purchases like this been made in the past?
- Will you need to seek approval from anyone else in the organization?

**Budget authority**

- Would this be coming out of your budget or is there someone else who controls the
  budget?
- Do you have a sign-off limit that you'd like to stay under?

**Blockers and blast radius**

- Who else would be impacted by this decision?
- Who could potentially veto this decision?
- Will we need to meet with legal? Procurement? Finance? Other departments?

**The playback close**

> "So let me play this back to you. It sounds like [role/name] will need to be brought
> into the conversation? Is that right? Is there anyone else who needs to be
> involved?"

Note how different these are from "anyone else you'd bring in?" That is a
participation probe. These ask who signs, who controls budget, and who can kill it.

**Veto power surfacing late is a named failure point in the Commit phase.** If it was
not squashed here, it shows up when there is no room left to handle it.

## Plan question bank (verbatim from the deck)

- What steps do you suggest we take moving forward?
- Would you be willing to introduce us to the main person (use names if possible) in
  Procurement so we can get the process started?
- Is there anyone else we should include in our next steps?
- When do you think we can meet with [role/name]?
- How much time should we allow for procurement/legal review?
- Is there anything else you think you'd need to see before moving forward? Or, is it
  fair to say that if we complete the steps we've outlined in this list, you will be
  in a position to move forward with an agreement?

That last one is the commitment test. It converts a list of steps into an agreement
about what happens when the steps are done.

## Proposal (Stage 3)

### The entry gate

You pivot to Proposal only once **the QP formula is fully built out**:

- The OAT tool is a yes to everything
- Every Power and Plan question in the decision tree is answered

`QP = VMD x Value x Power x Plan` is multiplicative, so an unanswered Power question
is a zero, and a zero means there is no qualified prospect to write a proposal for.

> *OAT is referenced in the deck but not defined in this repo. Confirm what it stands
> for and where it lives before relying on it.*

### Pricing before Proposal stage

- **Ranges are fine** in conversation before the opportunity is in Proposal stage.
- **A physical proposal only goes out once the opportunity is formally moved into
  Proposal stage.** The stage move comes first, the document second.

### Entrance criteria

Proposal presentation is done and pricing has been **socialized, not negotiated**.

### Exit criteria

All of these:

- Proposal submitted
- Price and terms verbally agreed by the decision maker
- CPQ built and delivered
- MAP with reverse timeline documented **in writing**
- Procurement and legal timelines understood

## Commit (Stage 4)

**Goal:** secure signature and PO so an invoice can be generated.

### Key actions

- Align with procurement and legal for final approvals
- Establish the contract execution timeline
- Update SFDC

### Named failure points

- Not understanding procurement and legal timelines
- **Not questioning vacations.** People being out is a real timeline risk and it gets
  skipped when setting dates
- Veto power surfacing late, because it was not squashed during Power questioning

### Entrance criteria

Negotiation complete, proposal agreed, verbal or email commitment obtained.

### Exit criteria

Signed agreement and/or PO, onboarding call invite scheduled, counselor assigned.

## Account Transition

**Relevance to the SMB motion is an open question** (see CLAUDE.md Section 12). This
applies when Acquire sells a new account and it transitions to an Engage rep.
Onboarding must happen within 30 days, at Service Kick-Off.

### Sequence

1. **Transition Form**, completed by the outgoing rep before the internal meeting
2. **Internal Transition Meeting**, outgoing rep, CSM, incoming rep, before any client
   meeting
3. **Warm Hand-Off Call**, outgoing rep, incoming rep, the client decision maker, CSM
   optional
4. **Email introductions**, sent by the outgoing rep with the incoming rep CC'd

For brand new accounts the Acquire rep is not needed on the warm hand-off call. The
point is speed, while the new member is still excited.

### Messaging on a rep change

Frame it as an addition, never a handoff. Sample line from the deck:

> "I want to introduce you to Paul. Paul has been added to your account management
> team as he brings great experience in your industry."
